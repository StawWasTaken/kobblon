# What Kobblon is for

Staw's notes, written down so they decide things rather than get
remembered differently by each of us. `neoclassic.md` says what Kobblon
looks like and `direction.md` says what it is building; this says what it
is **for**, and when it disagrees with either of those, this wins.

Five commitments. Each has what it means in practice, because a value
nobody can act on is decoration.

---

## 1. A 3-D toy with Duolingo's spine and a fever dream's face

Three things held at once, and the tension between them is the product.

**3-D** is what you make and where you go: low poly, primitive, free,
somewhere you stand inside rather than scroll through.

**Duolingo's spine** is the structure, not the mascot and not the
confetti: a thing that knows you, that has a shape to come back to, that
rewards turning up, that makes progress visible and small enough to start.
Quests, streaks, levels - the gem accent exists for exactly this and for
nothing else. What we are **not** borrowing is the nagging, the guilt, the
fake urgency or the engagement machinery behind it.

**A fever dream's face** is the part that stops it being a textbook.
Kobblon is allowed to be strange: colour that is too much, a mascot with
opinions, an interface that is a little dreamlike, a joke somewhere nobody
expected one. If a screen could belong to any company, it belongs to none.

The three are not alternatives. A screen that is only strange is noise; a
screen that is only structured is a bank. Everything carries some of each.

## 2. No corporate fluff, and no fake anything

**Corporate fluff** is any sentence written to sound like a company rather
than to tell somebody something. "Empowering creators to unlock their
potential" says nothing. So do: seamless, robust, leverage, elevate,
delve, ecosystem, solution. Words on Kobblon are what a person would
actually say.

It is also the shapes that come with it: the testimonial nobody gave, the
logo wall, the metric on the landing page that is there to look big, the
"trusted by" strip, the cookie banner written by a lawyer.

**Fake likes, and fake everything else.** No seeded numbers, no padded
counts, no rounded-up member totals, no activity invented to make a quiet
page look busy. If three people liked a World, it says three. If nobody
has, it says nobody has, and that is a true sentence about a new platform
rather than a failure.

This is checked rather than assumed: today nothing in the codebase
fabricates a count. The only randomness on the site is identifiers and
falling snow. It stays that way.

**And no fake functionality**, which is the same rule one level down: a
control that implies something the platform does not do is worse than no
control. Say what is not built yet, in the interface, not only in a
comment.

## 3. Simplify, heavily

The simplest things stay fun the longest. That belief is the whole of
Kobblon's shape, and it is also the hardest rule to keep, because every
individual addition looks reasonable.

What it means when building:

- **Only what a week-old platform needs.** Not a feature because a big
  platform has one.
- **The second version of anything is a problem.** Two upload dialogs, two
  greens meaning yes, two words for the same object - that is how a
  platform stops making sense, and it never happens on purpose.
- **Cut before adding.** A page that gained a section should usually have
  lost one. If something has three ways in, two of them are wrong.
- **Fewer words.** Every screen, every card, every button. The short
  version is almost always the honest one as well.
- **Limits are the creative part.** Low poly, few parts, a small palette.
  Somebody who has never built anything can start, and somebody who has
  built for years is made to be inventive. That is not a compromise on the
  way to realism; it is the point, and a hyper-real engine is boring by the
  second evening.

## 4. Made by people - that is the whole difference

Roblox is becoming a place where the things you play were generated and
the people you meet might not be people. Kobblon is the other thing, and
it is the clearest reason for Kobblon to exist at all.

**Worlds, items, avatars, clips, posts and names are made by human beings.**
Not prompted, not generated, not bulk-produced. The Creator Hub is built
for somebody sitting down to make something, and every part of it - the
Workspace, the uploads, the badges, the analytics - is for a person doing
that.

**Where AI is allowed:** keeping people safe. The moderation function
reads what is posted and helps a human decide. That is the only place it
touches the platform, it never has the last word on an account, and the
line that has been true since the day it was written stays true:
**warnings and suspensions only - AI cannot delete an account.**

**Where it is not allowed:** making the things. No generated Worlds, no
generated items, no generated avatars, no written-for-you descriptions, no
AI "creator assistant" that produces the work somebody came here to do.
If a feature's pitch is that you need less skill and less time, it is
probably the wrong feature for this platform.

This is a thing to say out loud, not only to practise. It is the sentence
somebody chooses Kobblon over Roblox for.

## 5. No bots, no fake content

A platform's numbers are a claim about how many people are there. Kobblon's
have to be true.

- **No bot accounts.** Not for seeding a new area, not to make a Community
  look joined, not to make the forum look alive, not for a screenshot.
- **No automated posting, liking, following or playing**, by us or by
  anybody else. An account that does it is suspended, and that is a rule
  the platform enforces rather than a line in the terms.
- **Nothing pretends to be a person.** Kobblon's own account posts as
  Kobblon. No sock puppets, no staff second accounts dressed as players.
- **The house account is marked as the house account** and is not counted
  among the people.

The cost is accepted: Kobblon will look quiet before it is busy. A quiet
place that is real grows into a loud one that is real. The other way round
does not work, and everybody can tell.

---

## Using this

When a decision is unclear, it is usually one of these five that answers
it, in this order: is it honest, is it simpler, is it made by a person, is
it strange enough to be Kobblon, does it have a shape somebody comes back
to.

When something here is wrong, change this document and say why. What must
not happen is the product quietly drifting away from it while the file
stays as it was.
