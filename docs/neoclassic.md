# Neoclassic

A Kobblon word, defined by Staw. It decides what gets built and what a
thing looks like when it is, so it is written down rather than remembered
differently by each of us.

## What it means

**It rejects corporate minimalism.** The black and white enterprise look,
built by companies optimising a metric, is the thing Kobblon is not. A page
that could belong to any company belongs to none, and Kobblon is not a
dashboard.

**It is the simplicity of the 2000s and 2010s web.** Early web games and
digital toys, unpretentious and immediately playable. The belief underneath
it: the simplest things stay fun the longest, and a hyper-real engine is
boring by the second evening.

**Low poly, primitive, free.** Basic shapes, few parts, clear structure —
K6 and the part system are this on purpose. It keeps the whole thing
accessible to somebody who has never built anything, and creative because
of the limits rather than despite them.

**A modern twist, not a copy.** It is not nostalgia cosplay. It takes the
soul of early 3D web platforms and rebuilds it with a modern independent
hand, so it feels remembered and still native to Kobblon.

## What follows from it, when building

- **Copy the system, not the look.** Where another platform has solved a
  social problem — friend requests, badges, passes — take the *mechanism*,
  which people already understand, and draw it in Kobblon's design. Making
  up a novel mechanism so as not to look like a copy is how a platform ends
  up with something worse that nobody recognises.
- **Only what a week-old platform needs.** Features that would make sense
  on a platform that started last week and wants people to play and build.
  Not a feature because a big platform has one.
- **Nothing sterile.** Colour, a mascot, words written by a person. If a
  screen could be a settings page at a bank, it is wrong.
- **No fake functionality, ever.** A button that does nothing is worse than
  no button. Say what is not built yet.
